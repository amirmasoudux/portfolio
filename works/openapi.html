— The Challenge

A technically superior product
that executives couldn't buy into

Finnotech had built one of the most capable Open API platforms in the region — a technically sophisticated system ready for enterprise-scale integration. But the sales process was stalling at the executive level.

The people making purchasing decisions weren't engineers. They were C-suite leaders and founders who needed to understand strategic outcomes, not technical capabilities. The gap between what the product could do and what buyers could understand was costing deals.

Sales materials caused cognitive overload in non-technical executives
No ROI framing — buyers couldn't calculate the value of integration
Identical messaging for enterprise C-suite and SME founders with completely different needs
Technical documentation positioned as the primary entry point
No visual representation of how integration would look in practice
"The platform was selling technical features when buyers needed to see strategic outcomes."
Persona 01 — Enterprise
The Risk-Averse Executive

Needs strategic value without technical jargon. Their question is "What does this mean for our business?" not "How does the API work?" They need outcomes mapped to their KPIs before the engineering team ever gets involved.

Persona 02 — SME
The Resource-Constrained Founder

Needs immediate reassurance about security, time, and cost. "Will this break what I already have?" comes before "What can I build?" They need a fast path to confidence, not a deep technical evaluation.

— The Strategic Pivot

From selling features
to selling outcomes

The entire design strategy rested on one reframe: stop explaining what the APIs do and start showing what they make possible.

Before
Selling Technical Features

Code samples, endpoint documentation, integration guides. Every touchpoint assumed deep technical context that most buyers didn't have — and didn't need.

→
After
Selling Strategic Outcomes

ROI visualisation, business scenario mapping, co-creation tools. Every touchpoint started with the buyer's world and worked back to the technology that enabled it.

— The Solution

Two tools that changed
the conversation

Digital ROI Simulator

A white-labeled interactive prototype that let buyers model their own integration scenarios. Input current transaction volume, output projected cost savings and new revenue. No technical knowledge required — the language of business, not engineering.

Printed Co-Creation Wireframes

A set of physical design cards used in workshop settings to map buyer-specific integration flows. Tangible, visual, and collaborative — designed to make abstract API capabilities concrete within the buyer's own business context.

Post-Launch Adoption Programme

A structured 6-month support plan built into the sales agreement: dedicated sandbox access, a CSR onboarding programme, and committed engineering support hours. Reduced perceived risk by guaranteeing accountability beyond the signed contract.
