Making the Abstract Tangible

Finnotech sold Open Banking APIs to banks and businesses. The product was strong, but executives couldn't see what it would do for them. I helped turn technical features into business outcomes they could see, touch and build with.

The problem

A strong product that buyers couldn't picture

Finnotech's Open Banking APIs were competitive, yet B2B conversion stayed low. The product wasn't the problem. The sales material described technical features, so non-technical leaders couldn't see the return on investment, and they didn't commit.

Context for European readers: Finnotech is an Iranian fintech that offered bank APIs to third parties, similar to what PSD2 enabled for banks and fintechs in Europe.

The barrier

Sales teams sold technical features, not business outcomes.

The impact

Buyers couldn't see the ROI, so deals stalled.

The goal

Redesign the sales story and materials so the value is obvious.

Research

Finding the confidence gap

Low sales were the symptom. To find the real blocker, we interviewed the whole team in person, including the sales representatives and the product manager.

PRIORITY 1 · ENTERPRISE

The risk-averse executive

Needs the strategic value without technical jargon. When something is unclear, they stay quiet and drop off rather than risk looking uninformed.

"What does this mean for our business?"

PRIORITY 2 · SME

The resource-constrained founder

Sees high risk in security, time and cost. Needs quick, concrete proof that the integration is safe and doable.

Prototypes

Double-sided service cards

A deck of printed cards, one per service. The front explains the service. The back shows a white-labeled mock-up of the client's own product after the integration, so a CEO could see exactly what their customers would experience.

01 / 04

Recreated in English from the original Persian cards. App names and amounts are examples.

In the meeting, and after it

Sales reps and clients laid the cards out together to design the client's solution in the meeting. Afterwards the cards stayed with the client as a brochure, which helped them remember and build consensus internally.

At an industry event, the building-block idea became physical: visitors built with real bricks and sketched on the cards.

Impact

Trust is a design problem

We validated the materials in two stages before rolling them out: first inside the sales process, then in the market.

COMMERCIAL

70% conversion uplift

Conversion of enterprise clients rose by 70%.

COMMERCIAL

Faster pipeline

Fewer early drop-offs and noticeably faster movement through the pipeline.

MARKET

4 more banks

Four banks reached out on their own to integrate the API list. Finnotech moved from a single provider toward an industry leader.

MARKET

Clients got excited

We tested the tools in real sales meetings. Clients "got excited" when they saw their own improved product.

Strategic outcomeTrust is a design problem. Once it is solved, it drives growth across the industry.
What happened next

Removing the remaining doubts

The first interviews showed that startups and SMEs had their own concerns. We focused on enterprises first and planned to return to this segment in the next phase.

IMPLEMENTATION

Free engineering help

Clients worried the integration would be too complex, so we offered six months of free engineering support.

SOCIAL RESPONSIBILITY

Free APIs for startups

New startups got selected API packages for free in their first six months.

FUNCTION

A free sandbox

For clients unsure whether the product would work for them, we introduced a free sandbox to test in.
